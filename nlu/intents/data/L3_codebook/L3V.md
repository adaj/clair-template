## Codebook for Valence Monitoring Cognition (L3V) Classification 

### 1. Negative Valence Monitoring Cognition
**Criteria:**
   - Expresses feelings of inadequacy, self-doubt, or negative emotions towards one's abilities or situation.
   - Indicates a lack of confidence, feelings of being overwhelmed, or negative self-assessment.
   - May include phrases like "I can't manage this", "This is too hard for me", "I'm not confident", etc.
   - Includes expressions of worry, frustration, or disappointment in relation to personal performance or outcomes.

**Examples:**
   - "I'm really struggling with this concept; it's not clicking."
   - "I keep trying, but I'm not making any headway."
   - "I'm feeling a bit unsure about leading this section."

### 2. Positive Valence Monitoring Cognition
**Criteria:**
   - Conveys confidence, proficiency, or positive feelings about one's abilities or situation.
   - Indicates self-assurance, enjoyment, or a positive outlook regarding performance or outcomes.
   - Uses phrases like "I'm good at this", "I can handle it", "I'm confident in my skills", etc.
   - Can include expressions of pride, enthusiasm, or satisfaction in one's own abilities or in the progress made.

**Examples:**
   - "I've got a good handle on this chapter; it's pretty interesting."
   - "Data analysis is my strong suit; I'm pretty confident with it."
   - "Our progress is exciting; we've really turned things around!"

### 3. Neutral Statement
**Criteria:**
   - Does not explicitly express positive or negative self-assessment or outlook.
   - Can be factual, procedural, exploratory, or observational in nature without emotional valence.
   - Often includes factual statements, general inquiries, or neutral clarifications related to the learning task.
   - May involve statements about logistics, schedules, resources, or task-related information.
   - Avoids phrases that directly express personal emotions, abilities, or self-judgments.

**Examples:**
   - "What's the next step in our experiment?"
   - "We should schedule a meeting to discuss our findings."
   - "Can someone clarify the objective of this task?"

### Guidelines for Coders:

1. **Context Consideration:** Always take the context of the sentence into account. Valence can often be context-dependent.

2. **Emotion vs. Fact:** Distinguish between statements expressing emotions or self-assessments and those conveying factual or procedural information.

3. **Subtlety in Language:** Pay attention to subtle cues in language that may indicate a lean towards positive or negative valence, even if not explicitly stated.

4. **When in Doubt, Choose Neutral:** If a sentence could reasonably fit into more than one category or if the sentiment is not clear, classify it as a neutral statement.

5. **Consistency is Key:** Apply these criteria consistently across all data to ensure reliability and coherence in coding.
