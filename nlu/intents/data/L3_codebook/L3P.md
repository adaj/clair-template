## Codebook for Individual/Shared Perspective (L3P) Classification

### 1. Individual Perspective
**Criteria:**
   - Expresses personal views, beliefs, or opinions.
   - Indicates a singular perspective, focusing on the speaker’s own thoughts.
   - May address others' opinions directly but maintains a distinct personal stance.
   - Often uses phrases like "I think", "In my opinion", "I believe", "From my point of view", etc.
   - Includes statements where the speaker is contrasting their views with others.

**Examples:**
   - "I personally think it's a good idea to explore alternative solutions."
   - "You don't get what I'm saying; my argument is different."
   - "In my experience, early planning prevents issues."

### 2. Shared Perspective
**Criteria:**
   - Conveys a collective viewpoint or consensus.
   - Indicates a shared understanding or agreement among a group.
   - Uses phrases like "We believe", "Our consensus is", "Together, we think", etc.
   - Can include expressions of collective decisions, common goals, or joint observations.
   - May involve stating the group’s perspective as a unit.

**Examples:**
   - "We think it's a good step to involve more stakeholders."
   - "Our joint opinion is that this strategy will yield better results."
   - "As a team, we're leaning towards a more agile approach."

### 3. Neutral Statement
**Criteria:**
   - Does not explicitly express personal or shared perspectives.
   - Can be factual, procedural, informational, or observational in nature.
   - Often includes statements of fact, neutral questions, or clarifications.
   - May involve restating information or summarizing content without personal input.
   - Includes sentences that focus on data, logistics, schedules, or neutral descriptions.

**Examples:**
   - "The next meeting is scheduled for Thursday at 3 PM."
   - "This chapter covers the fundamentals of the theory."
   - "The guidelines suggest using two methods for the experiment."

### Guidelines for Coders:

1. **Context Consideration:** Always consider the full context of the sentence. The classification depends heavily on the overall meaning rather than isolated phrases.

2. **Look for Key Phrases:** Use the listed criteria and examples as guidance. Key phrases and sentence structure are strong indicators of the intent category.

3. **Consistency is Key:** Apply these criteria consistently across all data to maintain reliability and accuracy in coding.

4. **Nuanced Interpretation:** Pay attention to nuances in language. Some sentences may seem to fit into more than one category, but subtle differences can guide correct classification.

5. **When in Doubt, Choose Neutral:** If a sentence is ambiguous or does not clearly fit into 'individual' or 'shared' perspectives, classify it as a neutral statement.
